Who I am

About

I'm a mechanical engineering student at UT Austin, going into my senior year with a minor in robotics. Most of what I do sits at the intersection of hardware and control — I've spent the last two summers at Boeing designing satellite flight hardware, from an 8-foot payload lifting system to a 12-layer PWB backplane, and I run PID and kinematics experiments on a robotic arm I built on my own time.

Outside of internships, I'm a dynamics engineer on Longhorn Baja Racing, where I've been optimizing brake rotor geometry for an off-road vehicle, and I spent a year and a half on Texas Guadaloop's pod team designing pneumatic braking systems for our hyperloop competition entries. Before that, I led the mechanical side of UT IEEE's Robomaster team, building autonomous defensive robots with a small crew of engineers.

I like projects where the math has to actually work — where a kinematics model or a tolerance stack-up either holds up under a real physical system or it doesn't. That's usually where I end up spending my time, whether it's for an internship, a competition, or something I wanted to understand better.
